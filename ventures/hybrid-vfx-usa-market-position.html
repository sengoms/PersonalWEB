Executive summary

Hybrid VFX USA should enter Atlanta as the low-cost, studio-credentialed prep vendor for a market that has shifted from tentpoles to volume, and it can do so for a $100K seed.

  • The market moved in Hybrid's favour. Georgia film and TV spend fell to $2.0B in FY26 (July 2025 to June 2026), a ten-year low, while the production count rose from 245 to 280 and independent production grew 83%. Marvel's decade at Trilith ended with Thunderbolts in 2025. Today's buyer is a series, unscripted or independent producer who decides on price and turnaround.
  • Hybrid's edge is studio credentials at offshore cost. Department heads with credits delivered at ILM, Framestore, Scanline and Sony Imageworks; roto, paint and matchmove at $70 to $110 an hour against $175 to $230 in Atlanta; overnight delivery because Chennai works while Atlanta sleeps; a TPN Gold Shield facility, status valid to August 2027. Few small Indian vendors can show the last two together.
  • Four segments, in this order. Atlanta post houses, brands and agencies first (NDA only, shortest cycles); independents and TV movies alongside; VFX-house overflow from day one on the Gold Shield; streamer series in Year 2 through the houses that win them. The first 90 days target 40 named buyers and are sized to produce 2 to 3 paid pilots by Month 6.
  • The ask is $100K, not $150K. A Georgia Tech ATDC membership with one secured review room cuts the office line from $44K to $90K down to $13K, and the Atlanta GM is hired at Month 7 against first revenue. Two tranches: $60K at incorporation, $40K at Month 7 against gates. On the revenue plan, about $62K of the seed is still in the bank at Month 12.
  • The Georgia post-production credit is a Year-2 lever, not a Year-1 pitch. It is earned on Georgia payroll and spend by the Georgia post company; Chennai work does not qualify.

Three decisions for the Hybrid Studio board: approve the $100K seed via ODI in two tranches; incorporate Hybrid VFX USA, Inc. as a Delaware C-Corp foreign-qualified in Georgia, 75% owned by Hybrid India; commit Shalini, Bharathi and Ashique as named supervisors for the first three pilots.

Market context: Georgia in FY26

Georgia's production economy is smaller than in 2022 but busier, and its mix has moved toward the work Hybrid sells.

  • Spend is at a ten-year low. Film and TV projects spent about $2.0B in Georgia in the 12 months to 30 June 2026, down from $2.3B the year before and less than half the $4.4B peak of FY22 (AJC, July 2026).
  • The count is up and the mix is lighter. Productions rose from 245 to 280 year over year, with more lower-budget independent films and fewer big-budget movies (AJC, August 2026); independent production grew 83% (FanBolt).
  • The tentpoles left. Marvel had no films slated for Georgia after Thunderbolts (2025) and moved its base to London (AJC, May 2025). Trilith's stages now carry Superman: Man of Tomorrow and Warner Bros. Television; the Georgia slate in autumn 2026 is series (Will Trent, Guilty Creatures, The Challenger, Heartland, Thunder Road, Tulsa King), unscripted (American Idol, The Circle, Family Feud) and independents.
  • Infrastructure and incentives held. More than 4M sq ft of purpose-built stages (Trilith, Tyler Perry Studios, Cinespace, EUE/Screen Gems, Assembly), a crew base built over 20 years, and the 30% uncapped production credit. Service vendors felt the slump: Quixote wound down its Atlanta rental operation in spring 2026 (CBS Atlanta).
  • Activity swings month to month. The Georgia Film Office's count of active productions fell to 13 in July 2026, the lowest since the pandemic, and recovered to 23 by August; the chart below shows the run since December 2025.

The count swung between 13 and 33 in nine months: a vendor that depends on a few large shows feels every dip, and one that sells to many small ones does not.

What it means for Hybrid: in a down market, buyers trade down on price and up on turnaround, and the incentive no longer brings the tentpoles that kept Atlanta's VFX houses full. Both favour a vendor priced 55 to 65% below local rates that can deliver overnight.

Channel ladder. Warm introduction first (IACC, TTE, GPP, Moonshine, FeTNA), which roughly doubles the reply rate; then events attended rather than sponsored (GPP monthly mixers, Post Atlanta 2027, Atlanta Film Festival, SCAD AnimationFest, SIGGRAPH Atlanta, Atlanta Ad Club); then LinkedIn and IMDbPro messages to the named role; then email. Three touches over three weeks, then stop and recycle in a quarter.

Three hooks that get a reply. Name their show and their shot type. Offer the overnight free test: ten shots sent at 6 pm Atlanta, back at 8 am, in their pipeline, no charge. Send a one-page rate card they can forward, because buyers forward prices, not decks.

Low-cost reach, done once in Month 1. Listings on the Georgia Film Office production directory, ProductionHub, IMDbPro and LinkedIn; a Vitrina listing (its claim that studios source there is self-reported); a weekly LinkedIn post of a Georgia production note or a shot breakdown.

The funnel below sizes the first 90 days: 40 named Tier-1 buyers produce about 14 replies, 8 free tests and 2 to 3 paid pilots, which is the Month-6 gate.

The free test is the step that does the work: it turns more than half of replies into evaluated work, and the paid pilot then rests on quality rather than on the pitch.

Economics

Year 1 costs $99,100 on the shared-office path, rounded to a $100K ask, and the seed covers the whole year even at zero revenue.

Pricing. Roto $70 to $85, paint $75 to $90, matchmove $80 to $100, roto-animation $90 to $110 an hour, against Atlanta averages of $175, $195, $210 and $230. Flat packages for independents and TV movies: Starter (10 shots, about $6K), Feature (25 shots, about $14K), and a per-episode retainer for unscripted. A rush multiplier; 50% deposit for independents, net-30 for houses and agencies. The free overnight test is ten shots, one night, no exceptions.

The shared office is where the saving comes from. A dedicated 1,500 sq ft office with fit-out runs $44K to $90K for 12 months; the ATDC membership, a desk and one locked review room run $13.1K. The content-security requirement is met by the review room, not the open desk, and all shot work stays in Chennai.

Two tranches, one gate. $60K at incorporation covers legal, the office stack and six months of operating cost at about $5K a month. $40K at Month 7 is released only if the Month-6 gates are met: two paid pilot clients, at least $10K in deposits received, one accepted batch, realised contribution margin of 28% or better, and a qualified pipeline of $100K. The board's exposure to a failed pilot is $60K.

Cash through the year. On the plan's $220K of Year-1 revenue from Month 4 at a 28% contribution margin (after India delivery cost, rework and commission), the lowest cash balance is about $28K in Month 3 and about $62K of the seed remains at Month 12. At zero revenue the seed still covers the year, with $900 to spare.

The dip to Month 3 is the office stack and the review-room build landing before revenue starts; the step in Month 7 is the second tranche arriving once the gate is passed.

Three-year trajectory (from the case study): revenue $220K, $1.4M and $3.2M; costs $100K, $950K and $2.05M; EBITDA $120K, $450K and $1.15M; active clients 3, 12 and 22; U.S. headcount 1, 3 and 6. The parent recovers the seed inside Year 1 on this arithmetic. The budget model's 28% contribution view is more conservative, showing Year 1 as a net cash use of about $37K, and the board should read both.

Year 2 is where the shape changes: 12 active clients and three U.S. staff, with the parent's seed already recovered.

Recommendations and the first 90 days

The board is asked for three decisions, and the plan that follows has one gate, at Month 6, that protects the second tranche.

  1. Approve a $100K seed via ODI in two tranches. $60K at incorporation; $40K at Month 7, released only against the Month-6 gates: two paid pilot clients, $10K or more in deposits received, one accepted batch, realised contribution margin of 28% or better, and a qualified pipeline of $100K.
  2. Incorporate Hybrid VFX USA, Inc. A Delaware C-Corporation, foreign-qualified in Georgia, 75% owned by Hybrid Studio (India), domiciled at ATDC Tech Square, with the Atlanta GM hired at Month 7 against first revenue.
  3. Commit leadership bandwidth. Shalini as production liaison for the first three pilots; Bharathi and Ashique as named supervisors in client material; a board sponsor for a monthly review.

What Hybrid India does in Month 1. Book the Gold Shield renewal assessment (status expires 19 August 2027, inside Year 1); sign the IP licence and the intercompany agreement; nominate the board sponsor; release five breakdown shots on own plates for the reel.

What the U.S. side does in the first 90 days:

  • Days 1 to 30: conflict-of-interest disclosure filed; incorporation and Georgia qualification; bank account; ODI Form FC; ATDC application; directory listings; vendor pack (W-9, insurance, NDA, security summary); 40 named Tier-1 targets in the tracker; first 20 touches.
  • Days 31 to 60: ATDC desk and the secured review room; 40 touches cumulative, 6 replies, 4 free tests; first two pilot clients signed from the post-house, brand, unscripted and independent pipeline; GM candidates shortlisted.
  • Days 61 to 90: first paid shots delivered; first case study published; Atlanta Film Festival, SCAD AnimationFest and SIGGRAPH Atlanta attended; 60 touches, 12 replies, 6 tests, one paid pilot.
  • Day 90 to Month 6: 100 touches; second paid pilot; $10K of deposits; first repeat order; $100K qualified pipeline; the gate.
  • Months 7 to 12: GM on board; overflow tests at scale with the Atlanta VFX houses; third and fourth clients; Tier-2 introductions through the awarded houses; $220K Year-1 revenue plan.

Missing the gate is a result, not a failure: the board keeps $40K and a working set of Atlanta relationships, and the pilot stops at $60K of exposure.

KPIs, reported weekly to the board sponsor. New named contacts; first touches; reply rate; free tests run; test-to-paid conversion; paid pilots; deposits received; qualified pipeline in dollars; realised contribution margin; average turnaround in hours.
